# Busy all day — am I growing?

[English](01-busywork.en.md) | [简体中文](01-busywork.zh-CN.md) · [Index](README.md)

> Practical heuristics, not scientifically validated universal conclusions. Adapt to authority, relationships, and constraints.

You handle messages, last-minute requests, and coordination, yet struggle to name what you have learned. Those tasks are not automatically worthless.

## Separate three possibilities

- **Maintenance:** familiar work that keeps things running; useful, but not necessarily a new learning opportunity.
- **Judgment practice:** similar tasks now require independent prioritization, exceptions, or tradeoffs.
- **Recurring drain:** the same problem returns without room to change the process or a reusable lesson.

These can coexist. Repetition alone does not establish stagnation.

## A small experiment

Choose three recent tasks. For each, note who used to help, what you decided this time, and what you could reuse. No skill score is needed.

If one small process is within your authority, capture a checklist and try it once more. Budget roughly ten to twenty minutes if your energy allows.

## What to observe

Look for earlier problem recognition, more independent judgment, or fewer unnecessary exchanges. If nothing changes, you may need feedback, resources, or different opportunities; do not automatically blame effort.

## Limits

Repetition can be necessary in a new role. Stability can be a valid goal during low-energy periods. Check your authority before changing a shared process.

**Try asking:** “Here are three things I keep doing. Help me distinguish learning from maintenance.”
