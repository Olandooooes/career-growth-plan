# How do I make overlooked work visible?

[English](03-invisible-work.en.md) | [简体中文](03-invisible-work.zh-CN.md) · [Index](README.md)

> Practical heuristics, not scientifically validated universal conclusions. Adapt to authority, relationships, and constraints.

Coordination, maintenance, and helping colleagues can be hard to describe in a review. First distinguish unclear communication from a mismatch between your work and what the reviewer values.

## Describe one contribution

Capture the problem, your personal action, the observed change, and supporting evidence. Without metrics, describe a deliverable, feedback, scope, or a specific change.

Fictional example: “I documented the handoff and helped a new teammate complete their first workflow.” This is more specific than “helped colleagues”; it does not justify adding “cut training costs by 50%”.

## Choose a suitable conversation

Use an existing one-to-one or work update to describe the contribution briefly, then ask: “Does this work align with your expectations? Where would you like me to take more responsibility next?” Not every action needs publicity, and team results should not become personal claims.

## A small experiment

Choose one experience from the past two weeks. Write three sentences and use them in your next suitable conversation. Allow roughly ten minutes to prepare. If public updates are uncomfortable in your environment, try a private conversation first.

## What to observe

Does the other person understand your role more accurately, offer specific feedback, or clarify expectations? Clearer expression does not guarantee rewards or solve every resource-allocation problem.

**Try asking:** “I coordinated much of this work but wasn't the project lead. Help me describe my contribution accurately.”
