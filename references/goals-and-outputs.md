# Goals, judgment, and career materials

Read when assessing work, planning growth, or producing career materials. Use only the relevant section. Follow the language policy in SKILL.md.

## Minimum useful profile

Gradually learn the actual role, nearest goal and deadline, available experience, and constraints on time, energy, and resources. Ask only for missing information that changes advice. An unclear career goal is acceptable: start with a concrete recent difficulty.

An optional, playful intake can ask:

1. What is your job title, and what do you actually do each day?
2. What recently made you think “this needed someone like me”?
3. What drains you most?
4. What would you most like to change in the next three months?
5. How much time do you want to spend trying something different each week?

The result is a provisional work profile and a next step, not a fixed personality label.

## Is this job working for me?

Identify the dimensions the user cares about: compensation and stability, learning, future options, autonomy, energy, and life outside work. Compare dimensions without inventing weighted job-value scores.

Separate the work environment from personal capability. Limited authority, resources, or feedback can constrain results. Salary comparisons require region, role level, date, and credible sources; do not infer market value from a short quiz.

For stay-or-leave decisions, outline actual options, known tradeoffs, important unknowns, and one reversible way to learn more. Do not assume a job change is best.

## Performance review / 述职

Confirm audience, period, expected length, and priorities only when not already known. Adapt to the user's process, including probation reviews or quarterly self-assessments.

Organize around objective → personal contribution → observed results and evidence → problems and next steps. Separate planned, attempted, and achieved work. Include maintenance and coordination without claiming hypothetical prevented losses as measured savings. Put unresolved facts after the usable draft so invented placeholders cannot be mistaken for final metrics.

## Promotion

Map actual next-level expectations to experiences, evidence, and gaps. If employer criteria are missing, label any framework as generic and not authoritative for that company.

Consider scope, independent judgment, influence, and reusable contributions; more work is not automatically next-level work. Suggest an opportunity within realistic responsibilities and resources, noting support needed. Do not invent promotion odds.

## Job change and interviews

Select experiences against the target role or supplied job description. Without a target, prepare general accomplishment material rather than claiming a role match.

Resume bullets describe personal action, its object, and observed result. Use verifiable deliverables, complexity, or feedback when metrics are absent. Avoid keyword stuffing.

Interview stories cover context, task, personal action, results, and reflection. Add a few relevant follow-up questions. Remove or confirm model-supplied details before using them as facts in practice.

Classify requirements as supported by experience, adjacent but needing validation, or currently unsupported. Do not fabricate match scores or employment promises.

## Growth actions

Use a real experience to identify one useful gap related to the user's goal, then propose one to three ordered actions. Start with a one- or two-week experiment unless a longer plan is requested; longer plans need assumptions and checkpoints.

For each action, make clear what to do, its link to the goal, estimated effort, a deliverable or observable change, and when or under what condition to review it. Estimates must fit the user's available time.

Example: after recurring requirement changes, spend roughly half an hour reviewing three incidents, draft a confirmation checklist, and try it in the next change discussion. Observe whether disagreements surface earlier; do not promise a percentage reduction in rework.

At review, separate execution, usefulness of the method, and changes in external conditions. If it did not help, change the action or hypothesis instead of simply increasing workload. Respect choices to maintain stability or reduce strain.

## Entrepreneurship, only when requested

Look for testable opportunities among recurring problems: who else experiences them, existing alternatives, willingness to pay, and a low-cost validation step. Personal competence is not proof of demand. Start with interviews or a small sample, not a recommendation to resign. Employer secrets and internal customer lists are not assets to sell.
